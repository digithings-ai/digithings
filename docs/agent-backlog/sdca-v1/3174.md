## Goal

This is the issue where the strategy stops being a hand-authored personality and becomes a calibrated
one. The owner's requirement — *do nothing around fair value; buy progressively harder as it gets
cheaper; sell progressively harder as it gets richer* — fixes the curve's **shape**; what it does not
fix is **where the dead zone sits, how wide it is, and how steep each side gets.** Those are the free
parameters, and guessing them is what the presets currently do.

## Two things that make this different from optimizing a Slapper

**1. The objective cannot be total return.** Maximizing return on a DCA book drives every parameter
to one degenerate corner: buy everything on day 1 (which is lump-sum, not DCA) in any up-trending
asset. Whatever objective is chosen must reward the *signal*, not the market. Proposed primary:

```
maximize  vs_flat_dca_pct        # did the valuation signal beat blind averaging?
subject to:
  capital_deployed_pct >= floor  # a strategy that never deploys can't lose, and isn't a strategy
  max_drawdown_pct     <= cap
```

`vs_flat_dca_pct` (from #3171) is the right primary because flat DCA already captures the averaging
effect — anything above it is attributable to the valuation model. Report `vs_lump_pct` alongside but
do not optimize it. **Justify the final choice in the PR body**; this is the single most consequential
decision in the issue and should not be inherited from this text unexamined.

**2. Overfitting is the default outcome, not a risk.** Six shape parameters plus indicator weights
against one asset's single price history — with roughly one and a half cycles of usable BTC data —
will fit noise if allowed to. Mandatory:

- **Walk-forward, not a single split.** Fit on a window, evaluate on the next, roll forward. Report
  per-fold out-of-sample results, not just the aggregate.
- **A held-out final segment** that is not touched until the parameters are frozen.
- **Report the in-sample/out-of-sample gap.** A large gap is the finding — publish it rather than the
  flattering number.
- **Sensitivity:** if a 5% perturbation of the winning parameters materially changes the result, the
  optimum is a spike and should be rejected in favour of a stable plateau.
- The rails themselves are fitted on full history (#3173), which leaks future information into any
  backtest evaluated on that same history. **Either refit rails per fold or state the bias explicitly
  and bound it.** Do not quietly ignore it — this is the most likely way for the published number to
  be wrong.

## Scope

1. `strategy_specs.py` entry for `sdca` — bounds for the six shape params (#3169) and indicator
   weights, so `digiquant_run_optimize` can reach it.
2. Walk-forward harness over the SDCA path, reusing NautilusTrader for every evaluation (CLAUDE.md:
   Nautilus is the sole backtest engine — `SdcaBacktestReport` is a parity harness and must not become
   the optimizer's fitness function).
3. Objective + constraints as above, configurable, with the chosen default justified.
4. Persist the winning parameters as a new preset (`btc_optimized`) carrying its fit window, folds,
   objective, and OOS results as provenance.
5. A written result: what was searched, what won, in-sample vs. out-of-sample, sensitivity, and the
   honest verdict on whether the signal beats flat DCA.
