## Goal

A **research spike, not an implementation issue.** Its deliverable is a written recommendation plus
the implementation issues it justifies filing — or a documented decision not to file them.

SDCA's crypto valuation rails rest on a thesis — long-run power-law growth with cyclical deviation —
that equities do not share. An index does not have a genesis date or a decaying power law; it has
earnings, rates, and sentiment. Applying the crypto provider to SPY would produce rails that look
plausible and mean nothing. So before any equity SDCA is built, establish **what "cheap" and
"expensive" mean for equities, and whether we can source it reliably.**

## Candidate metrics (owner's list, plus the obvious neighbours)

| Metric | Note |
|---|---|
| PE / forward PE / CAPE (Shiller) | CAPE has the longest history and the clearest cheap/rich interpretation |
| Buffett indicator (market cap / GDP) | FRED has both legs — and FRED is **already wired** via `digiquant_get_macro_series` |
| Put/call ratio | CBOE; sentiment rather than valuation — likely a *separate indicator* in the composite, not a rail |
| Equity risk premium (earnings yield − 10y) | rate-aware, which raw PE is not |
| Credit spreads (HY OAS) | FRED; a regime filter more than a valuation |

## Questions the spike must answer

1. **Sourcing.** For each metric: a free or low-cost source, its update cadence, its history depth,
   and its licensing/ToS. Prefer official APIs over scraping; flag anything needing human review
   before we depend on it. **FRED first** — we already have that integration, and a metric we can get
   from FRED is worth more than a marginally better one behind a fragile scrape.
2. **Level vs. rails.** Crypto SDCA positions price *within fitted rails*. Does the same shape work
   for CAPE (position CAPE within its own historical distribution), or do equities want a different
   mapping to a `[-3, 3]` z? Answer concretely — this decides whether `RiskModel` fits equities at all
   or needs a sibling abstraction.
3. **Cadence mismatch.** GDP is quarterly and revised; earnings are quarterly and lag. A daily SDCA
   decision reading a quarterly series must not imply precision it does not have, and must never read
   a value before its real-world publication date. **Point-in-time correctness is the trap here**:
   using a revised GDP figure at a date before the revision existed is lookahead bias that will
   silently flatter every backtest. State how it is avoided.
4. **Does it work?** A quick historical check: would a CAPE-driven or Buffett-driven SDCA have
   accumulated near 2009 and 2020 lows and distributed near 2000 and 2021 highs? If the answer is
   unconvincing, that is a legitimate and valuable finding — say so.
5. **Which assets.** Broad index ETFs (SPY/VTI) plausibly; single stocks need per-company fundamentals
   and are a much larger scope. Recommend a boundary.
