## Goal

SDCA currently runs on exactly one asset because `BtcPowerLawRiskModel` is the only `RiskModel`. Ship
the generic provider so the same strategy runs on ETH, SOL, and other assets with cyclical behaviour
and no bespoke valuation model.

## The reuse that makes this small

`btc_power_law.py` is already 90% generic machinery: quantile regression per rail, rearrangement to
stop rails crossing, coefficient persistence, convergence and history-length guards. Only two things
are BTC-specific:

- the time basis is `ln(days_since_genesis) − μ` (a *genesis* date only BTC meaningfully has), and
- the fit is quadratic in log-time, which encodes BTC's specific decaying-power-law thesis.

So this is a **refactor plus one new provider**, not a second implementation. Extract the shared
fitting core, then parameterize the time basis and the basis functions. Resist the temptation to copy
the module and edit it — two divergent copies of quantile-rail fitting is the outcome to avoid.

## Scope

1. **Extract the shared core** from `btc_power_law.py` into a fitting module: rail fitting,
   rearrangement, convergence/history guards, persistence. `BtcPowerLawRiskModel` becomes a thin
   configuration of it. Its behaviour and its existing tests must not change.
2. **`GenericValuationRiskModel`** — rails from log-price against a fitted long-term trend, with the
   time basis anchored to the asset's own first cached bar rather than a genesis date. Support both a
   log-linear and a log-quadratic trend, selected per asset and recorded in the coefficients file.
3. **Short-history degradation.** ETH and SOL have far less history than BTC, and SOL less than ETH.
   Two distinct mechanisms, and the issue must not conflate them:
   - **refuse** below a minimum history threshold (already the pattern, `MIN_FIT_HISTORY_DAYS`);
   - **widen** the rails when history is thin but sufficient, so a poorly-constrained fit produces a
     *less confident* signal rather than a confidently wrong one. A model fitted on 2 years should not
     emit the same rail spacing as one fitted on 12.
4. **A rolling-z fallback** for assets where even a trend fit is unjustifiable — log-price z-score
   against a rolling window, which degrades to "cheap relative to recent history" rather than
   pretending to know a long-run trend.
5. Extend the `risk_model` selector in `digiquant_build_sdca_risk_index` (#3168) to reach the new
   providers.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Shared fitting core extracted; `BtcPowerLawRiskModel`'s existing tests pass unchanged.
- [ ] `GenericValuationRiskModel` produces non-crossing rails on ETH and SOL daily history.
- [ ] Rails widen measurably as fit history shortens — pinned by a test on truncated history, not
      just asserted in prose.
- [ ] Providers refuse with a clear reason below the minimum-history threshold.
- [ ] Rolling-z fallback works on a short synthetic series.
- [ ] `build_risk_index()` runs against each provider via the selector.
- [ ] `pytest -m unit -k "riskmodel or sdca"` passes; `make score` clears all four dimensions.
