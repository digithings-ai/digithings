The same modules make different applications. A use case is a choice of
modules, not a separate platform.

### Financial research

A quant workflow needs market data, a research graph, backtesting and a
readable result. digiquant supplies the vertical and its provenance rules;
digigraph routes the work; digisearch finds the documents around it; digichat
is where the analyst asks and reads. Run it on a laptop for one analyst, then
add capacity when the research team grows.

### Voice

Voice belongs on the machine that hears it. digivoice handles speech,
transcription and rewriting locally, while digichat provides the conversation
surface. The audio does not need to leave the building to become useful.

### Retrieval and RAG

A private corpus needs ingestion, indexing and answers that cite their source.
digisearch owns retrieval, digivault holds the documents and digillm connects
the model. The same stack runs against a folder on a laptop or a shared index
for a department.

### Agents and exploration

An agent needs tools, a loop and a record of what happened. digigraph runs the
loop, MCP describes the tools, and digitrace keeps the trail. Add your own
service as another tool and it joins the environment without changing the hub.

### From one person to an organisation

These are not starter demos with an enterprise edition behind them. The same
contracts hold at every size. Identity, tracing and data become more important
as the team grows, but the application does not become a different application.

> **Why digithings.** You can prove the whole application on your own machine,
> then place it where it needs to run. The modules stay the same. That is what
> makes the stack useful to a solo builder and to an enterprise at once.
