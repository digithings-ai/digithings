---
title: "Growing the stack, not the bill"
order: 2
summary: >-
  A rented service grows your invoice with every extra user. A self-hosted
  stack only grows the part that actually needs to.
---

Rented infrastructure scales by multiplication: more seats, more tokens,
more requests, each multiplied by a price someone else set. The bill tracks
your growth exactly, which sounds fair until you notice it tracks your
*success* just as precisely — the better things go, the more it costs to
keep them going.

A self-hosted stack scales by addition instead. A module that is small and
replaceable can be given more hardware, or replaced outright, without
touching the modules around it. Growth is a series of local decisions —
add a worker here, a faster disk there — rather than one global number that
rises no matter which part of the system actually needed the help.

The practical effect is that most of the stack can stay exactly as it is
while only the part under real load changes shape. That is a property of the
architecture, not a promise about any particular workload, but it is the
reason "scale" does not have to mean "renegotiate the contract."
