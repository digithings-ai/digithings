---
title: "Where the ceiling actually is"
order: 3
summary: >-
  The limit on a self-hosted stack is your hardware and your operational
  discipline — not a price sheet you have no say in.
---

Every rented service has a ceiling, and the ceiling is wherever the vendor's
pricing or policy decides to put it. It can move without warning, and when
it does, the decision is theirs.

A self-hosted stack has a ceiling too, but it is a different kind: the
hardware you have, and how well you run it. That ceiling is real — there is
no infrastructure model that makes capacity free — but it belongs to you. You
can raise it by adding hardware, improve it by operating more carefully, or
choose to live under it for a while. None of those are a vendor's decision to
make on your behalf.

That is the actual claim of this chapter, stated plainly: self-hosted
infrastructure does not remove limits, it relocates them, from a contract
you did not write to a system you can see the inside of.
