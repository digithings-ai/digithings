---
title: "Two kinds of scale"
order: 1
summary: >-
  Usage scale and organizational scale are different problems, and a stack
  that is honest about the difference solves them separately.
---

"Scale" quietly means two different things, and conflating them is how a
reasonable design decision turns into an unreasonable constraint.

**Usage scale** is more requests, more data, more concurrent work. It is
largely a hardware and software-engineering problem: faster storage, more
compute, better caching, a queue instead of a loop.

**Organizational scale** is more people, more teams, more rules about who
can see what. It is largely a policy problem: identity, permissions, audit
trails, and the ability to add a new team without re-architecting the whole
system around them.

A platform that only solves the first kind of scale still falls over the
moment a second team wants to use it. A platform that only solves the second
kind is bureaucracy wearing infrastructure's clothes. The two need separate
answers, even inside one stack.
