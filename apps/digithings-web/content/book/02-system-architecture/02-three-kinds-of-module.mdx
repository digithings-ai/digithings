---
title: Three kinds of module
order: 2
summary: >-
  Services run, libraries are imported, surfaces face people. Telling them
  apart is the key to reading the rest of the architecture.
---

The most common misreading of digithings is to treat every module name as the
same kind of thing. They are not, and the difference matters for how you deploy,
scale and replace them.

**Services** are long-running processes with a network interface. They have
an address, a health check and their own lifecycle: digigraph, digisearch,
digiquant, digikey, digivault, digitrace and the digichat back end. You deploy
a service, you scale a service, and you can put another implementation behind
the same interface.

**Libraries** have no address at all. digillm, digibase, digifetch and digiclaw
are packages that services import. They never listen for traffic, so there is
nothing to deploy and nothing to secure at the network level; they travel
inside the services that use them. When a library improves, every service that
imports it improves on its next release.

**Surfaces** are what people see: the digichat interface, the dashboards, the
terminal apps and the marketing sites. They hold no business logic of their
own. A surface asks the hub, and the hub decides.

| Kind | Has a network address | You deploy it | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Service | yes | yes | digigraph, digisearch, digiquant, digikey, digivault |
| Library | no | inside a service | digillm, digibase, digifetch, digiclaw |
| Surface | yes, for people | yes | digichat, the dashboards, the terminal apps |

This split is what makes the stack modular in practice rather than in name. A
team that only needs model access can import digillm and stop there. A team
that already has a retrieval system can leave digisearch out and point the hub
at its own tool server instead.
