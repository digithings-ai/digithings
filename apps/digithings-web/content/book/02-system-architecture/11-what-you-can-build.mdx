  The same parts make a research assistant, a domain product like digiquant,
  or an agent platform of your own.
---

The architecture is general on purpose. digiquant, the finance vertical, is the
proof: it is an ordinary digithings vertical that adds market data and research
tools, and everything else it needs (identity, the hub, model access, memory,
the chat interface) comes from the shared stack.

You can follow the same pattern. A **research assistant** needs only the hub,
retrieval, the vault and digichat. A **domain product** adds one vertical
service with your tools and data, and inherits identity, tracing and the
interface. A team that already has its own agent can skip the hub and use the
pieces it lacks, such as digillm for model access or digivault for memory, as
plain libraries and services.

Whatever you build, you start the same way: run the whole stack locally, prove
it end to end on your own machine, then decide where it should live.

> **Why digithings.** You adopt it a module at a time. Open source, runs on your
> own hardware or on Cloudflare, standard protocols at every boundary, and no
> part you cannot replace.
