---
title: "What you can build"
summary: "The same parts support a research assistant, a domain vertical of your own, or an agent product built on top — and the choice between them is mostly about how much you want to own."
---

The architecture is modular in a practical sense: the modules are separable in
the order you would expect, and so are the products you can build from them.
There are three shapes, and they differ mainly in how much you want to own.

**A research assistant.** This is the smallest useful thing here, and the one the
system is arranged to make easiest. You get the hub, the retrieval vertical, the
model layer and the chat surface. You connect a corpus, and you have an assistant
that searches your material, reasons over it and shows its work through tracing.
Almost nothing in the product layer is yours, because almost nothing needs to be.

**A domain vertical of your own.** This is where the design pays off. You write
one service that owns a capability your domain needs — insurance claims,
clinical guidelines, a proprietary dataset — and expose it through the open
protocol. The hub discovers it at runtime, so your capability becomes available
to every surface that talks to the hub, with no change to the hub itself. You own
one service. You do not own routing, identity, model selection, tracing or the
chat surface.

**An agent product.** This is the same thing with a product surface on top. If
you are building something people use directly, the chat surface is already
written, already handles identity, and already knows how to reach everything the
hub can reach. What remains is your own product logic and your own vertical or
verticals.

The honest difference between the three is not capability — it is how much you
want to operate. A vertical you write is a service you run, secure and keep
alive. That is a real cost, and it is the point at which the hosted option
becomes worth comparing against self-hosting rather than a detail to defer.

The one thing that does not change between them: whatever you build, it is
yours. The modules are open source under a permissive licence, the protocols are
open, and nothing in this chapter requires a commercial arrangement to run. The
hosted path is a convenience with usage-based pricing, not a prerequisite.

> **Where to start**
>
> Self-host, run the verification the system expects before a release, and
> connect one vertical of your own. That is the shortest path from reading this
> chapter to owning something, and it is the same path the system's own first
> vertical took.
