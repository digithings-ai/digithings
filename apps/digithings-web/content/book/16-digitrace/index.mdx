A system that cannot explain itself cannot be operated. When an answer looks
wrong, someone has to be able to ask which tools ran, in what order, what each
one saw and how long it took. digitrace is how digithings answers. Once switched on, it records the trail of every request as it happens, so nobody has to piece
it together from log lines afterwards.

The trail serves several readers. An operator uses it to debug a bad answer.
A reviewer uses it to audit a research number. Anyone deciding whether to
trust the system can see the steps behind a result. And once tracing is switched on, the trail shows where to improve: a tool that takes four seconds, or a call
that fails one time in ten, stands out at once.

## How it works

digitrace has two faces. As a library, it gives every service a thin helper
that records when tracing is configured and costs nothing when it is not.
Services adopt it without depending on any tracing vendor. It also reports
whether tracing is active and healthy, so the hub and the
dashboards can show its status without touching sensitive configuration.

```mermaid one request, one story: the trail follows it across every service it touches
flowchart LR
  REQ[request] --> HUB[hub steps]
  HUB --> VERT[vertical steps]
  VERT --> TRAIL[one trail]
  TRAIL --> OP[operator]
```

The trail follows a request across service boundaries, so a step in the hub
and the step it triggered in a vertical appear in one story rather than in
two logs that have to be matched by timestamp.

## Where it fits

Everything that acts is traced. The hub records routing and tool calls, the
verticals record their pipeline stages, and the model layer records usage
through its hooks. Because the library is vendor-neutral, switching to a
different tracing backend changes configuration, not service code.

The trail goes wherever you point it: a backend on your own machine, one
beside your cloud workers, or one on your own servers with the retention you
choose. Tracing every request costs some storage and a little time per request.
That costs far less than debugging a multi-service answer from scattered
logs, and you never pay another vendor to watch your own system.
