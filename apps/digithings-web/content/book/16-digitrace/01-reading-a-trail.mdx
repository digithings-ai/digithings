---
title: Reading a trail
order: 1
summary: >-
  A walk through one recorded trail, and what each entry tells the operator
  who follows it.
---

A trail is read the way it was written: in order, step by step. It opens
with the request — what was asked and under which identity. Then come the
steps: which tool ran, what it was given, what it returned, how long it
took. Tool calls nest where one step triggered another, so a hub step and
the vertical step it caused read as parent and child rather than as two
unrelated lines.

The entries an operator looks for first are the unusual ones: a step that
took ten times its neighbours, a tool that appears where it never did
before, a retry that succeeded after failing. The trail makes those visible
without interpretation — it records durations, attempts and order as facts,
and leaves conclusions to the reader. When a result is disputed, the trail
is the exhibit: not an explanation of what happened, but the record from
which explanations are built.
