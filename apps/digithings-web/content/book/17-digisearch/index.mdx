Language models know a great deal and remember nothing of yours. Everything
an organisation knows, its documents, its history, its earlier research, sits
outside the model. digisearch brings it in. It takes in your documents,
indexes them by meaning and finds the right passages when a question needs
them.

Without retrieval a model answers from training memory: fluent, confident
and sometimes invented. With it, the answer arrives with the passages it
stands on, and a sceptical colleague can check them. digisearch makes that
the default shape of every answer that touches private knowledge.

Passages go to whichever model answers, so confidential work belongs on a
local one.

## How it works

Two paths stay strictly apart. Ingestion splits documents into passages,
turns each into an embedding, a list of numbers that places text by meaning,
and stores them in an index, so a question about staff turnover finds a
passage that only says attrition. Answering takes a question, runs a keyword
search and a meaning search together, reranks the results and hands the best
passages to the model with the question, so the model reasons over supplied
evidence instead of recalling.

```mermaid two paths share an index but never logic: indexing cannot change how a question is answered
flowchart LR
  DOC[documents] --> ING[ingest]
  ING --> IDX[(index)]
  Q[question] --> RET[retrieve]
  IDX --> RET
  RET --> ANS[cited answer]
```

The paths share storage and nothing else, and they need different
permissions: a search key can ask questions and cannot add documents. When
a workspace is set, the service adds its filter to every query, so one index
can hold several workspaces and one team can't read another's documents,
even by asking. The catch is that nothing is searchable until someone
ingests it.

## Where it fits

digisearch sits beside digiquant as one of the hub's research arms: quant
work answers from market data, search answers from documents. The chat leans
on it for grounded answers and the research pipeline calls it for
background. Ingestion runs on demand, and either way it lands
in the index the answering path reads.

The index backend is one setting: Chroma on local disk by default, pgvector in
Postgres, Cloudflare Vectorize or Azure AI Search. In every home, ingestion is
an explicit act, so nothing enters the index you did not put there.
