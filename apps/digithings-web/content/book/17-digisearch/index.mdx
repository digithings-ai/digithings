Language models know a great deal and remember nothing of yours. Everything
an organisation knows, its documents, its history, its earlier research, sits
outside the model. digisearch brings it in. It takes in your documents,
indexes them by meaning and finds the right passages when a question needs
them.

Without retrieval a model answers from training memory: fluent, confident
and sometimes invented. With it, the answer arrives with the passages it
stands on, and a sceptical reader can check them. digisearch makes that the
default shape of every answer that touches private knowledge.

## How it works

Two paths stay strictly apart. Ingestion splits documents into passages and
stores them in an index built for meaning, so a question about staff turnover finds a passage that only says attrition. Answering takes a question, retrieves the
passages most likely to matter and hands them to the model with the question,
so the model reasons over supplied evidence instead of recalling.

```mermaid two paths share an index but never logic: indexing cannot change how a question is answered
flowchart LR
  DOC[documents] --> ING[ingest]
  ING --> IDX[(index)]
  Q[question] --> RET[retrieve]
  IDX --> RET
  RET --> ANS[cited answer]
```

The paths share storage and nothing else. A document being indexed cannot
change how a question is answered, and answering never writes to the index.
A workspace filter can ride along with every query, so one index can hold
documents for several workspaces.

## Where it fits

digisearch sits beside digiquant as one of the hub's research arms: quant
work answers from market data, search answers from documents. The chat leans
on it for grounded answers and the research pipeline calls it for
background. Ingestion runs on demand, and either way it lands
in the index the answering path reads.

The index backend is the only thing that changes between homes: an embedded
index on a laptop, managed vector storage in the cloud, whatever fits the
corpus on your own hardware. In every home, ingestion is an explicit act, so
no document crosses a boundary you did not approve.
