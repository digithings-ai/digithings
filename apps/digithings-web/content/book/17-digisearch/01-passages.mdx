title: Passages, not pages

summary: >-
  How documents become retrievable passages, and the judgement calls inside
  splitting text well.
---

Retrieval is only as good as what it retrieves, and models read passages,
not documents. Ingestion therefore splits each document into passages sized
for reasoning: large enough to keep context, small enough to stay on point.
That splitting — chunking — is the quiet craft inside the search vertical.

Three judgements shape it. Boundaries follow meaning — paragraphs and
sections rather than fixed character counts — so a passage says one thing.
Each passage keeps enough surrounding context to stand alone, because the
model meets it without the document around it. And overlap between
neighbours is deliberate: a small shared margin means nothing important
falls exactly on a cut. None of these has a universally right value; each
corpus tunes them by reading retrieval quality, not by theory.
