summary: >-
  How documents become retrievable passages, and the judgement calls inside
  splitting text well.
---

Retrieval is only as good as what it retrieves, and models read passages,
not documents. Ingestion therefore splits each document into passages sized
for reasoning: large enough to keep context, small enough to stay on point.
That splitting — chunking — is the quiet craft inside the search vertical.

Three judgements shape it. Boundaries follow meaning, so a passage says one
thing: the default chunker (Chonkie's semantic chunker) cuts where the topic
shifts rather than at a fixed character count. Each passage keeps enough
surrounding context to stand alone, because the model meets it without the
document around it. And where cuts are by size, the token and sliding-window
chunkers let neighbours overlap, so nothing important falls exactly on a cut.
None of these has a universally right value. The chunker is one setting, or a
line per index, and each corpus tunes it by
reading retrieval quality, not by theory.
