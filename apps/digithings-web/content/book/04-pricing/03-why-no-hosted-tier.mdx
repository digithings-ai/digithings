---
title: "Why no hosted tier"
order: 3
summary: >-
  A hosted tier would rebuild the exact dependency this book argues
  against — so the absence of one is a position, not an oversight.
---

It would be easy to add a hosted tier: run the stack centrally, charge
per seat or per request, and let people skip the self-hosting step
entirely. It would also quietly undo everything the earlier chapters argued
for, by putting someone else back between you and your own infrastructure.

A hosted tier does not just compete with self-hosting — it depends on
self-hosting being inconvenient enough that people pay to avoid it. That is
a fine business to be in, and plenty of good companies are in it honestly.
It is simply not this one's business, because the whole argument of this
book is that the inconvenience is worth absorbing.

So the absence of a hosted tier is not a gap in the roadmap. It is the
conclusion the rest of this book was building toward, priced.
