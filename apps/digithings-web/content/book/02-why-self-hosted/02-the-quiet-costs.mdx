---
title: "The quiet costs of someone else's stack"
order: 2
summary: >-
  Lock-in, silent price changes, and a roadmap you cannot see are not
  edge cases of renting infrastructure — they are the business model.
---

The costs of renting rarely show up as a single bad decision. They show up as
a slow accumulation of small ones, each reasonable at the time:

- **Lock-in.** Every integration you write against a vendor's shape is a
  little more expensive to unwrite later. Switching costs are not an accident
  — they are frequently the point.
- **A roadmap you do not see.** A feature you depend on can be deprecated,
  repriced, or quietly degraded, and you learn about it from a changelog
  entry rather than a conversation.
- **Your data, on their timeline.** Export tools exist, but they exist on the
  vendor's schedule and in the vendor's format, not yours.
- **Pricing that tracks your success.** The better the product works for
  you, the more you use it, and usage-based pricing means your costs grow
  exactly when switching away becomes hardest to justify.

None of this requires bad faith. It only requires that the incentives of the
party setting the terms and the party paying them are not the same incentives.
