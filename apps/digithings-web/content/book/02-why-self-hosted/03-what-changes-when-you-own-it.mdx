---
title: "What changes when you own it"
order: 3
summary: >-
  Owning the stack trades a slower start for a position: your hardware, your
  data, your choice of model, and no one else's throttle.
---

Running your own infrastructure does not remove cost. It moves it: from a
recurring fee that grows with your usage to work you do yourself, setting the
stack up, updating it and keeping backups. What you get for that work is
control over the three things below.

Your data stays on hardware you control, in a format you chose: notes as
markdown files, documents in an index you can open. Your choice of model is a
setting, so moving to a better or cheaper model the week it is released is your
decision, not a vendor's roadmap item. And the only throttle on your usage is
the hardware in front of you, not a plan tier someone else priced.

digithings is one stack built that way: bring your own model provider, run it
on your own machine or in your own cloud account, and nothing about how it
behaves depends on who pays the bill. It is not the only way to self-host AI
infrastructure. Whichever you pick, check the same three things: who holds the
data, who picks the model, and who sets the limits.
