---
title: "What changes when you own it"
order: 3
summary: >-
  Owning the stack trades a slower start for a position: your hardware, your
  data, your choice of model, and no one else's throttle.
---

Running your own infrastructure does not remove cost. It moves it: from a
recurring fee that scales with your success to an upfront effort that scales
with your ambition. What you get for that trade is a position rather than a
subscription.

Your data stays on hardware you control, in a format you chose, for as long
as you choose. Your choice of model is a configuration setting, not a
migration project, so a better or cheaper model is a decision you make on
your own schedule. And the only throttle on your usage is the hardware in
front of you, not a plan tier someone else priced.

digithings is one example of a stack built around that position rather than
around a subscription: bring your own provider, run it on your own machine
or in your own cloud account, and nothing about how it behaves changes
depending on who is paying the bill. It is not the only way to self-host AI
infrastructure, and this book is not an argument for this project in
particular — it is an argument for the position digithings happens to take.
