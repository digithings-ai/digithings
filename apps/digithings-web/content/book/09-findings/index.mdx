A book written from design documents is also an audit of those documents.
Twice now, writing a chapter surfaced places where the written design and
the running system say different things. Each one became a ticket under the
book's epic, with the chapter section that surfaced it linked, so the review
loop is part of how the book gets written rather than a separate activity.

Findings come in two kinds, and they close differently. **Drift** means a
document went stale while the system moved on: the fix is to update the
document, and anyone can verify the fix by reading the two side by side.
**Decision** means the document and the system disagree about intent — whether
some work is planned or done, or which shape is meant to win. Editing prose
on a decision item produces a document that reads confidently and is wrong,
so those wait for a ruling first and only then get edited.

Each finding goes to the seat that owns the surface it describes, even when
the stale words sit in a document someone else touches. The owner of the
facts fixes the words; the owner of the page reviews them.

The loop also records its misses. Two candidates from the architecture
chapter turned out to be false: both were measured against an out-of-date
copy of the tree, and the documents were right. A process that only records
hits teaches its authors nothing about checking the current tree first, so
withdrawals stay in the record with the reason they failed.

The live list of findings lives on the board under the book's epic, where
each ticket carries its status. This appendix records the process, the
template every finding follows, and what has been found so far.
