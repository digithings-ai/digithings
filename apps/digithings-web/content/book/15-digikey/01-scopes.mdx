---
title: Scopes, the narrow grant
order: 1
summary: >-
  How each credential carries exactly what it allows, and why narrow grants
  keep incidents small.
---

A credential that can do everything is a catastrophe waiting for a leak.
Scopes are how the system avoids minting one. Every credential carries a
short list of named permissions — which services it may call, which actions
it may take — and every service checks the list before acting. A credential
for reading research cannot move funds, not because operators are careful,
but because the grant never included it.

Scope design follows two habits. Grants start narrow and widen on request,
never the reverse; it is easy to add a permission someone needs and nearly
impossible to notice one nobody needed. And scopes name business actions —
read research, run backtests, manage documents — rather than technical
operations, so an operator can reason about a grant without reading code.
