---
title: The approval moment
order: 1
summary: >-
  What happens when a tool needs a person, and how the design keeps approval
  meaningful rather than reflexive.
---

Some tools run unattended; some wait for a person. The approval moment is
the interface between the two: the system pauses, shows what it wants to
do and with what, and continues only on a yes. Designing that pause well
matters more than it looks — an approval a person grants without reading is
no approval at all.

Three habits keep it meaningful. Approvals name the action in plain words,
not tool identifiers — what will happen, to what, and what it costs. They
arrive with the context that justifies them, so the person decides from
evidence rather than from trust. And routine approvals can be pre-authorised
by policy while novel ones always ask, so attention goes where novelty is.
The gate records every decision either way, approved or declined, because
the record of what was allowed is as much a control as the asking.
