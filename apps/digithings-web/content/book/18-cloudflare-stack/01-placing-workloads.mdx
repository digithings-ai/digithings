---
title: Placing each workload
order: 1
summary: >-
  Which services become workers, which become containers, and the rule of
  thumb that decides.
---

Placement starts from a simple question: does this workload answer requests
or keep processes? Request-shaped work — interfaces, request layers,
scheduled runners — becomes workers that scale to zero and wake per
request. Process-shaped work — model runners, pipelines, tracing backends —
becomes containers that stay up and hold state in memory. The rule of thumb
holds surprisingly far: stateless request handling at the edge, stateful
processes in containers, durable facts in managed stores.

Scheduled work deserves a note. Runners wake on timetables, do their job
and stop — the cheapest shape on the platform, because idle time bills
nothing. Long research runs go the other way: start a container, let it
work, shut it down. Matching the workload to the shape is most of the cost
discipline; the billing follows the placement rather than needing separate
attention.
